# Ruhama 1946 — Bilingual Content Plan for 16:9 and 9:16
# רוחמה 1946 — תוכנית תוכן דו-לשונית ל-16:9 ול-9:16

**Audience:** general public online, Hebrew and English at parity — not a school group, not a museum visitor.
**Formats:** a six-part 9:16 series (social-first) and one 16:9 long-form web page.
**Anchor:** the 80th anniversary, 28 August – 2 September 2026.

---

## 1. The spine

One sentence, and everything hangs off it:

> **EN** — For six days in 1946, two thousand British soldiers took a two-year-old kibbutz apart looking for weapons. They broke the floors, slit every mattress, poisoned the food stores and burned the hay. They found nothing. Six weeks later that kibbutz launched eleven new settlements across the Negev.

> **HE** — במשך שישה ימים ב-1946 פירקו אלפיים חיילים בריטים קיבוץ בן שנתיים בחיפוש אחר נשק. הם ניפצו את הרצפות, קרעו כל מזרן, זיהמו את מחסני המזון ושרפו את המתבן. הם לא מצאו דבר. שישה שבועות אחר כך יצאו מאותו קיבוץ אחת-עשרה נקודות התיישבות חדשות בנגב.

**Rule for every screen:** the wreckage is the evidence, not the point. The point is that the search failed — twice.

---

## 2. The 9:16 series — *Six Days at Ruhama* / *שישה ימים ברוחמה*

Six episodes, one per day of the siege, published daily 28 Aug – 2 Sep 2026.
**45–60 seconds each. 1080 × 1920. Silent-first: every word on screen, voice-over optional.**

### Universal card grammar

| Card | Duration | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Hook | 0–3 s | One line, huge type, over a still. No context yet. |
| 2. Photograph | 3–12 s | Slow push-in on one restored image. Caption at the bottom third. |
| 3. Photograph | 12–24 s | Second image, cut on the beat. |
| 4. The detail | 24–40 s | A quoted line from the board — the members' own words, in quotation marks, on a plain field. |
| 5. Turn | 40–52 s | The fact that reframes the day. |
| 6. End card | 52–60 s | Series mark + "Day N of 6" + archive credit + follow prompt. |

**Safe areas:** keep all type inside the middle 1080 × 1420. Nothing important in the bottom 320 px (platform UI) or the top 220 px.

## 4. Design and language rules

**Typography**
- Hebrew: Frank Ruhl Libre for display, Heebo or Assistant for UI. English: the same Frank Ruhl / Georgia pairing so both languages feel like one object.
- Hebrew and English are never mixed on the same card. Two renders, one design.
- Set `dir="rtl"` on the whole document for Hebrew, not per-element. Mirror the layout, do **not** mirror the photographs.
- Numerals and Latin quotations inside Hebrew text (`YEHUDI SWINE`, `28.8.1946`) need explicit LTR isolation or they will scramble.

**Colour**
- Paper `#F6F2EA`, ink `#1C1A17`, one accent — the deep red of the exhibition board, `#8C3B2E`.
- Restored images stay black and white by default. The colourised versions are a separate, labelled set.

**Image handling**
- Every published image carries: *Kibbutz Ruhama Archive · 1946 · restored 2026*.
- Colourised images carry an additional, non-optional label: **"Colour added digitally — the original is black and white."** Never publish a colourised frame without it.
- No crops that remove the print's own edges on the long-form page — the object matters.

**Editorial**
- Never call it "the British" without a unit. Name the 6th Airborne Division.
- Never publish `29` (the blackboard) without its caption and never as a thumbnail.
- Where sources disagree, say so on the page. The dating discrepancy is a feature: it shows the work.
- Attribute the members' quotations to the kibbutz members, not to the archive.

**Accessibility**
- Burned-in captions on every 9:16 episode, in the episode's own language.
- Alt text in both languages, written from the catalogue's `subject` column.
- Contrast ≥ 4.5:1 for all body type over images — use a scrim, not a lighter font.

## 6. What still has to be true before any of this ships

1. Siege dates confirmed from a primary source. A six-day series with the wrong start date is worse than no series.
2. The originals rescanned. Phone photographs of a board are a starting point, not a master.
3. Archive sign-off on captions and credits. This is a community's own record of what was done to it.
