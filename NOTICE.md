This repository contains two very different kinds of material. They are **not** under the same licence.

## 1. The photographs — © Kibbutz Ruhama Archive

Everything in `images/` and `og.jpg` derives from photographs held by the **Kibbutz Ruhama Archive**, taken in 1946 and mounted on the exhibition board *"רוחמה במצור — חיפוש נשק תש"ו-1946"*. They are published here **by permission of the archive** and remain the archive's property.

**They are not licensed for reuse.** If you want to use one — in a book, a broadcast, an exhibition, a school project, a social post — ask the archive first. Do not scrape this repository for stock imagery.

Every image must be credited as:

> Kibbutz Ruhama Archive, 1946. Digitally restored 2026.

**Colourised versions** (not included in this repository) must additionally be labelled *"Colour added digitally — the original is black and white."*

## Before you publish this site

- [ ] The Kibbutz Ruhama Archive has seen the site and approved the captions and credits.
- [ ] The siege dates have been confirmed from a primary source (see the "what is still unknown" section — sources disagree between 28 August / six days and 25 August / seven days).
- [ ] Anyone identifiable in the photographs, or their family, has been consulted where they can be traced.

The third point matters most. Four people appear in these pictures. They were photographed on the worst week of their year, in their own home, without being asked. Treat them accordingly.

## The narration voice

The audio narration is read by a **synthesised voice**. It was generated by
ElevenLabs Voice Design from a written description of a *type* of speaker — an
elderly Israeli man, weathered, unhurried — and not cloned from any recording.
It is therefore no living or dead person's voice, and impersonates no one.

This was a deliberate choice. The obvious shortcut was to clone a well-known
poet's voice from a documentary. That voice is not ours to take: he died in
2018, consent would belong to his family, and a site whose text says *nothing
was invented* cannot publish an invented person reading it.

The site labels the narration as synthesised in every section, the same way it
labels the colourised photographs.

Where a real recording of a real person is used — Haim Gouri reading Alterman —
it is embedded from its original published source and credited, not reproduced.

Alongside the exhibition-board photographs, the site now carries images the
Kibbutz Ruhama archive published itself on **PikiWiki**, Israel's free-use photo
bank (organisation 8112). These are separately licensed and separately credited;
they are *not* covered by the "all rights reserved" line above.

| File | Subject | Date | Source | Licence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `images/childrens_house_june_1946.jpg` | The children's house after the search | 29 June 1946 | Kibbutz Ruhama Archive, via Harvard University | Public domain |

The original scans live in `images/sourced/`, untouched.

**One deliberate choice.** PikiWiki also carries a colourised, upscaled version
of the children's-house photograph (image 100872). We are not using it. The
colourisation smears the children's faces into blotches that are machine
invention, not photographic detail — and these are real children, photographed
after soldiers went through their nursery. The site uses the original black and
white scan (image 20721) instead. Same rule as everywhere else here: nothing is
invented, and where a machine touched an image we say so.

## Drawn illustrations (AI-generated)

`images/sketches/` holds eight ink drawings generated in 2026 with FLUX
(flux-1.1-pro, via Replicate). They illustrate events for which **no photograph
exists**: the 1912 founding, the well dug in 1913, the 1917 departure, the 1936
uprooting, the 1943 return, the night landing of 29 March 1944, the tracker dogs
of August 1946, and the eleven Negev points that October.
A second batch covers the people and the object of the search itself: a
**slik** (an arms cache beneath a floor), the weapons being carried out
beyond the perimeter before the troops arrived, Palmach training in the
wadis, the cordon closing on 28 August, and soldiers breaking up a floor.
Thirteen drawings in total.

They are **not** historical sources, and the site never presents them as such:

- every prompt is locked to pen-and-ink with visible hatching, so they can never
  be mistaken for a photograph at a glance
- no faces. Figures are small, distant, backs turned. We do not invent portraits
  of real settlers, and we do not invent faces for the four unidentified people
  in the archive photographs
- they live in their own folder, are excluded from the gallery of archive
  material, and carry a "Drawing / רישום" label plus a full disclaimer in every
  caption
- where a photograph exists, the photograph is used instead

`scripts/sketches.py` holds the prompts, so anyone can see exactly what was
asked for and regenerate or replace them.

## Ambient music

`audio/ambient.mp3` is an instrumental track generated on Suno by the site
owner. No lyrics, no sampled recording of any performer.

It is processed before use: the tail is crossfaded back into the opening so the
loop has no audible seam, and the whole thing is normalised to **-20 LUFS** —
four decibels under the narration's -16 LUFS, so it can never compete with a
voice.

In the player it is **off by default and never autoplays**. This site is about
a search of a children's house; music that starts on its own scores somebody
else's grief without being asked. The reader turns it on, the choice is
remembered, and it ducks to a quarter of its volume whenever narration plays.
