# Ruhama Under Siege, 1946 — Research Brief
# רוחמה במצור, 1946 — תיק מחקר

*Prepared 10 August 2026, for the 80th anniversary of the British weapons search at Kibbutz Ruhama.*

---

## 1. The one-paragraph version

**EN** — In late August 1946 the British Army threw a cordon around Kibbutz Ruhama and neighbouring Kibbutz Dorot in the western Negev. Around 2,000 troops, drawn from the 6th Airborne Division — known in Palestine as *ha-Kalaniot*, "the Anemones", for their red berets — sealed the settlements: no one in, no one out. For roughly six days they searched for the Haganah's arms caches. They smashed floors, dug trenches along the walls of buildings, slit every mattress, emptied the stores, mixed the food with chemical fertiliser, burned haystacks, wrecked the dining hall and the commune hut, and chalked "YEHUDI SWINE" on a blackboard. They found nothing. The weapons had been moved outside the perimeter before the troops arrived. The kibbutz took months to rebuild — and weeks later served as a launch point for the *11 Points* settlement operation that put Jewish settlement across the Negev.

**HE** — בסוף אוגוסט 1946 הטיל הצבא הבריטי מצור על קיבוץ רוחמה ועל שכנו קיבוץ דורות שבנגב המערבי. כאלפיים חיילים, מהדיוויזיה המוטסת השישית — המכונה בארץ "הכלניות" על שם הכומתות האדומות — סגרו על היישובים: אין יוצא ואין נכנס. במשך כשישה ימים חיפשו את סליקי הנשק של ההגנה. הם ניפצו רצפות, חפרו תעלות לאורך קירות המבנים, קרעו כל מזרן, רוקנו את המחסנים, ערבבו זבל כימי במזון, העלו מתבנים באש, החריבו את חדר האוכל ואת צריף הקומונה, ורשמו בגיר על לוח "YEHUDI SWINE". הם לא מצאו דבר. הנשק הועבר אל מחוץ לגדר עוד לפני שהגיעו. שיקום המשק ארך חודשים — ושבועות ספורים אחר כך שימשה רוחמה נקודת זינוק למבצע **11 הנקודות** בנגב.

## 2. Timeline

### Before the siege

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 1911–1913 | The Moscow society *She'erit Yisrael* buys ~5,000 dunam by the Arab village of Jammama and founds the Ruhama farm — the first modern Jewish settlement on Negev land. A well is dug in 1913; water at 38 m. |
| 1917 | The farm is destroyed in WWI fighting; the settlers are expelled by the Ottomans. |
| 1929 | Rebuilt, then abandoned again after the Tarpat (1929) riots. |
| 1932 | The *Gan-Shlomo* company buys much of the land and brings Jewish workers to plant orchards. |
| 1936 | The Arab Revolt: houses razed, orchards uprooted, the site abandoned a third time. The same year, Hashomer Hatzair garinim from Poland and Romania found Kibbutz *HaAmal* at Kiryat Haim. |
| 2 Dec 1943 | A first group of seven from HaAmal goes down to the Ruhama land, reopens the well, puts up the first huts. |
| 29 Mar 1944 | Official *aliyah la-karka* — carried out clandestinely at night, because British law barred Jewish settlement in the Negev. |
| 1944–1946 | Ringed by hills and hard to approach, Ruhama becomes a clandestine training ground: a secret Palmach and Haganah camp in the south of the kibbutz; weapons and explosives tested in the nearby wadis. Neighbouring Arab villagers hear the noise and report it to the British. |

### The wider 1946 confrontation

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 29 Jun 1946 | **Black Saturday** (Operation Agatha): mass arrests of the Yishuv leadership; the big search at Kibbutz Yagur uncovers a major arms cache. |
| 22 Jul 1946 | The King David Hotel bombing. |
| 30 Jul – 2 Aug 1946 | **Operation Shark** — cordon and search of Tel Aviv. |

### The siege of Ruhama and Dorot

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| **28 Aug 1946** | The cordon goes up around Ruhama and Dorot. ~2,000 troops. Radio Jerusalem announces the operation the same day *(the board carries a card citing "Radio Jerusalem, 28.8.1946")*. Three units of the 6th Airborne Division take part — the board records their formation numbers as **68, 941, 67**. |
| Day 1–2 | Everyone is held; supplies are not allowed in to the besieged settlements. Systematic search begins: floors broken, trenches dug along the walls. |
| Day 3–4 | Stores emptied; food mixed with chemical fertiliser; seed grain spoiled; haystacks burned; livestock scattered. The commune hut and its contents destroyed. |
| **Day 5** | *Mishmar* runs the story on its front page: **"Destruction and abuse in the besieged settlements — fifth day of the siege and searches at Ruhama and Dorot."** The British officer Wilson is quoted calling Ruhama **"the largest arms centre after Yagur."** |
| Day 6 | The search winds up. **Nothing is found.** |
| ~2–3 Sep 1946 | The cordon is lifted. |
| Autumn 1946 | Golda Meyerson (later Meir), acting head of the Jewish Agency political department, tells foreign journalists the searches were *"an invitation to destroy Ruhama for the third time."* |
| 5–6 Oct 1946 | Ruhama serves as a launch point for the **11 Points in the Negev** operation. |
| 1947–48 | Ruhama becomes a forward base for the Negev: field hospital, emergency airstrip, radio station, logistics centre. Children and mothers are evacuated. |

## 3. Open questions — flag these before publishing

1. **Exact start date and duration.** Two dating traditions exist:
   - **28 August – 2/3 September 1946, six days** — used by the Haganah heritage material and by regional accounts, and consistent with the board's own Radio Jerusalem card dated 28.8.1946.
   - **25 August 1946, seven days** — given by the Yad Ya'ari / Kibbutz Story account of Ruhama.
   The board's *Mishmar* clipping ("day five of the siege") should settle it once the clipping's own date line is read from a proper scan. **Do not publish a day-by-day series until this is resolved.**
2. **The unit numbers 68, 941, 67** are drawn on the board inside formation triangles. Confirm what they denote (brigade / battalion / field company) against the 6th Airborne Division order of battle.
3. **"Officer Wilson."** Full name, rank and role not yet established.
4. **Dorot's outcome.** Ruhama's caches were not found. Whether the same is true at Dorot needs checking — the two settlements are usually reported together.
5. **The Weizmann quotation** on the opening panel is not yet traced to a published source.
6. **Casualties.** No source consulted reports deaths at Ruhama. Confirm before saying "no one was hurt."

---

## 4. Why this story travels

- **A search that found nothing is a better story than a search that found something.** The drama is in the hiding, not the finding — and the punchline is already written.
- **It is unusually well documented from the inside.** The kibbutz photographed its own wreckage, kept the newspaper clippings, and wrote captions in the first person: *"Why did you destroy the baking oven? Do the officers not know that you cannot hide weapons in a lit oven?"*
- **It has a face.** The photograph of a young woman standing beside a British warning sign, and the woman salvaging utensils in the wrecked kitchen, carry the whole story without a word of commentary.
- **It has a hard, difficult object.** "YEHUDI SWINE" chalked on a blackboard. Handle it with context, never as a standalone shock image.
- **It has a sequel.** Six weeks after the British finished wrecking Ruhama, Ruhama was the jumping-off point for eleven new settlements across the Negev. The search failed twice over.
